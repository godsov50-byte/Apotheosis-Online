## Rarity is not authority

The proposed ordinal rarity lottery is a production outcome model, not a declaration of canonical rarity names. F23 maps skill and quality to weights summing to100; item identity and a registered deterministic creation draw bind the result. Revealing the same item again cannot reroll it. Canceling an unwanted result must not restore all spent materials and effort for another independent attempt.

The F23 proposal now explicitly uses the same normalized integer expression in data, executable behavior, prose and examples. Without the parity term, the five weights sum to100−(b mod2), which is99 for odd b. The existing declared policy allocates that single integer remainder to ordinal0 while preserving all other weights. For b19, [41,25,19,13,1] is invalid at total99; [42,25,19,13,1] totals100. This algebra and the integer/nonnegative/cap constraints justify retaining the pre-existing code behavior, rather than treating code as correct by fiat. Skill, quality and draw bounds are validated; the distribution remains a PROPOSAL, not canonical AO rarity.

## Originals, replicas and ceilings

A replica's rarity ceiling is independent of its allowed conceptual capability envelope. F44 compares energy, range, duration, targets, information depth and authority operation sets componentwise. Passing a scalar rarity check does not authorize a new operation. A same-Tier replica cannot improve a restricted original by optimizing tags, and a high-quality manufacture cannot copy a unique binding, personhood, sacrifice or legal ownership.

A ceiling redirects otherwise too-high rarity results under the explicit lottery policy; it does not create an original. Restricted items need a stable original ID, versioned blueprint channels, permitted envelope and reviewed copying rule. Disassembly/reassembly or batch renaming cannot manufacture a new unique identity or reset a production limit.

## Production, failure and conservation

F24 chooses the limiting whole-unit quantity from available material, funded labor and capacity. With100 material units at4 per unit,1000 labor seconds at50 per unit, and capacity100, output is20 units;80 material and1000 labor are consumed,20 material remain. Lowering material to20 instead permits5 units. These are proposed example units, not an established AO recipe.

Mass production uses batch/item identities; limited runs preserve cumulative quantities; one-of-a-kind production preserves the creation event and capability signature. Failure should leave explicitly accounted scrap, loss or work-in-progress, never unrecorded deletion followed by free replacement. The model demonstrates bounded operational profiles, not complete statistical calibration for every recipe and social scarcity condition.
